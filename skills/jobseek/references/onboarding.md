# Onboarding and ledger updates

Use with the [shared records](records.md) and [actual state commands](state-commands.md). This reference defines the conversation and evidence handling; the record contract defines field names and storage behavior.

## Choose the workspace and applicant

Keep reusable skills and private applicant data separate. Use the applicant's selected jobseek workspace; create one profile for each applicant and require an explicit profile identifier on every data operation. If there are several plausible profiles, ask which one to use before opening personal records. Do not reuse data from another profile or infer the applicant from a previous unrelated chat.

On first use, collect a display name and the minimum information needed for the requested task. Offer import from an existing CV/resume, publication list, certificates, portfolio, or other supplied documents. Do not scan the applicant's entire computer or connected services without task-specific authorization.

Record interests as the applicant states them: target roles and seniority, industries, research or functional interests, countries/locations, remote/hybrid/on-site preferences, relocation, travel, compensation preferences, and genuine nonnegotiables. Distinguish preferences from exclusions. Ask about eligibility only when relevant; never infer it from name, nationality, institution, or location. Missing answers stay unknown.

## Build detailed records

Capture the following when relevant, without making all fields mandatory:

| Area | Useful detail |
|---|---|
| Employment and education | Actual title or degree, institution, location, dates with precision, responsibilities, field, thesis or training focus |
| Research and projects | Question or objective, methods, scale, model/system, individual contribution, collaborators, outputs, supported results |
| Leadership and service | Formal or informal role, decisions owned, people mentored or coordinated, activities, verified outcomes |
| Technical skills | Exact method/tool, context of use, supported proficiency description, frequency or duration if known, last-used date |
| Achievements | Situation, task/responsibility, actions, results, attribution, constraints, supported metrics and units |
| Publications and presentations | Full citation, authorship, venue, date, identifier/link, and current status; distinguish preprint, submitted, accepted, and published |
| Patents | Inventorship, title, jurisdiction, number if known, filing/grant dates, and application versus granted status |
| Awards, scholarships, grants | Name, granting body, date, role, amount or selectivity only when supported |
| Certificates and licenses | Issuer, title, issue/expiry dates, credential identifiers or verification link when relevant |
| Other evidence | Teaching, mentoring, volunteering, memberships, languages, portfolio, and relevant coursework |

Use actual dates or preserve uncertainty such as year-only dates. Distinguish affiliation dates from employment dates. Do not convert a course into professional experience or a technique mention into expertise. Keep team achievements distinct from the applicant's own contribution.

## Import and review

1. Preserve identified source documents separately from the skill package. Record provenance and a locator such as page, section, paragraph, or certificate field. A reference to an unread source is not verification.
2. Extract factual assertions into pending records. Report uncertain OCR, ambiguous names/dates, incomplete citations, and contradictions instead of silently repairing them.
3. Present a compact review grouped by experience or source. The applicant may confirm a group, correct individual records, or leave entries pending. Preserve each decision's scope.
4. Explicit applicant additions/corrections can be user-attested immediately. If a correction resolves a contradiction, record the resolution and retain prior versions. Do not select between conflicting sources without evidence or applicant direction.
5. Only user-attested or confirmed records are usable for matching and drafting. Pending, conflicted, and retired material cannot silently become application claims.

Historical conversation context can help locate material but does not automatically confirm an applicant fact. For scientific accomplishments, preserve what the source actually demonstrates; distinguish computational predictions, planned validation, experimental observations, and publications. Verify external scientific claims using appropriate primary literature when needed.

## Ask useful evidence-gap questions

Prioritize gaps that affect a selected job or a strong accomplishment story. Ask a small related group rather than an exhaustive questionnaire. Examples:

- What part did you personally design, execute, or decide?
- What changed as a result, and what source supports that outcome?
- Is the result measured, qualitative, preliminary, or still planned?
- What scale, timeframe, collaborators, methods, and constraints help explain the work?

A supported qualitative result is acceptable. Never demand a percentage or invent one to complete STAR. Keep unresolved stories incomplete until clarified; a submission-ready document can omit an unsupported outcome or omit the story, subject to applicant review.

## Changes, feedback, and the readable dossier

Facts, career preferences, writing preferences, approved answers, and application decisions have different meanings. Record a wording rejection without modifying the underlying fact. Record global preferences only when explicitly expressed as global; otherwise use the relevant job/application scope. Do not infer the applicant's reason for rejection.

Use the shared helper to preserve revisions, validate profile identity, and refresh the dossier. If the readable dossier was manually edited, inspect the differences and reconcile them as proposed updates before overwriting it. Do not present a generated dossier as a second independent source of truth.

Flag materials that depend on a corrected fact for renewed review. End with a concise account of what changed, what is usable, and what remains pending or conflicted.
