# Job discovery, matching, and company research

Read [shared records](records.md), [state commands](state-commands.md), and [matching commands and scoring](matching.md). Do not substitute an improvised score for the shared algorithm.

## Scope and acquisition

Use the selected applicant's usable facts and recorded interests to form role, skill, industry, and seniority queries. Expand with truthful synonyms, adjacent roles, and transferable methods, while preserving stated nonnegotiables. Keep identifying or confidential project details out of public queries unless necessary and authorized.

Default to 20 distinct JD candidates per batch, unless the applicant chooses another number. A candidate is a distinct opening, not another syndicated copy. Record the requested scope, actual queries and sources, access outcomes, candidates processed, unfinished work, and when the search ran. A source-access failure does not prove that no jobs exist there.

Build a source plan from the applicant's field, locations, and stated constraints. Named websites are examples, not an exhaustive list or source limit. Unless the applicant explicitly narrows employer sectors, include relevant public-sector, private-sector, academic, and nonprofit opportunities. Choose sources across the following categories where relevant:

- General job boards and aggregators, such as Indeed, Glassdoor, LinkedIn, and Google Jobs.
- Direct employer career sites and their linked application systems, including employers discovered beyond the major boards.
- Government sources: for U.S. searches, check USAJOBS and relevant federal agency career pages, official state employment portals for each target state, and relevant county/city or other local government portals. For other countries, identify the corresponding official national and regional sources. Follow agency-specific hiring routes when listed rather than assuming one portal covers every opening.
- Universities, academic medical centers, hospitals, research institutes, national laboratories, and nonprofit organizations through their own career pages and relevant shared boards.
- Field-specific job boards, professional societies and associations, regional industry groups, and specialist recruiting or staffing sites when the employment type fits the applicant's scope.
- General web searches combining role/skill synonyms and locations to discover additional employers, niche boards, and postings missing from familiar sites.

Spread discovery across relevant source categories before filling the batch; do not take the first 20 openings from a single familiar board and call it broad coverage. Select candidates for full review based on the applicant's scope, without fixed per-site quotas. Record which sources and categories were checked, inaccessible, out of scope, or still pending, with reasons for omissions. Carry unfinished coverage into the continuation plan rather than implying the batch exhausted the market.

Use available web tools or an authorized browser. Verify newly discovered sources and follow listings back to an official employer or government destination where possible. Do not assume a platform has an accessible API. If access requires the applicant's login, hand over that step. Do not bypass a challenge or paywall; use an accessible official posting or applicant-supplied JD and record the limitation.

Read the full posting before declaring its requirements assessed. Preserve the JD text and links with retrieval time, available posting/update dates, job title, employer, location, requisition identifier, salary if stated, deadline if stated, and verification status. Do not infer a posting date from the retrieval time or a deadline from ordinary site text.

Prefer a verified employer career posting as the canonical application destination. Compare employer, requisition, role, and location to deduplicate. Do not merge different requisitions or locations simply because the titles match. Preserve alternate source links, and identify repost uncertainty rather than guessing.

## Normalize and assess requirements

Separate responsibilities, qualifications, technical skills, and keywords from eligibility/location/compensation constraints. Keep explicit required versus preferred language. Do not make an employer preference into an unstated legal or eligibility barrier.

For government openings, retain the announcement's stated hiring paths or eligible applicant groups, citizenship/residency requirements, grade or classification, specialized experience, clearance requirements, and application deadline when provided. Assess these against explicit applicant evidence; flag unknown eligibility rather than assuming qualification or excluding the role solely because the ledger is silent. Distinguish government employment from contractor roles.

- Count a repeated competency once. Group equivalent statements under a single normalized requirement.
- Count OR alternatives as one requirement satisfied through an accepted alternative, not several mandatory requirements.
- Do not score an umbrella requirement and its illustrative examples twice.
- Attach exact usable ledger fact identifiers to direct or transferable evidence. Transferable evidence also needs a brief explanation; similarity alone is not proof.
- Mark a confirmed lack of experience as a gap. Missing ledger information stays unknown. Pending imports cannot earn credit.

Use the shared helper's default weights and threshold: required 2, preferred/unclassified 1; direct 1, transferable 0.5, unknown/gap 0; include scores at or above 30% using the unrounded value. Present the unknown-weight share. Ask targeted questions if missing information could cross the threshold or materially alter a ranking.

Keep all qualifying discovered openings in one list, ordered by evidence coverage, then recorded interest alignment, then verified posting date (newest first; missing dates last), with a deterministic identifier tie-break. Show constraint conflicts even for high scores. Inclusion means worth review, not automatically eligible, recommended, or likely to be hired.

## Research every qualifying match

For each qualifying opening, produce a substantive brief covering the relevant employer or business unit and what is publicly known about:

- Products, services, research areas, or pipeline relevant to the role.
- Recent announcements, funding, expansion, new facilities, or location changes.
- Product/pipeline changes, reorganizations, partnerships, or other developments relevant to the work.
- The team's apparent remit and the actual wording of the opening.

For government, academic, and nonprofit employers, adapt the brief to the agency or institution's mission, programs, funding, and organizational context relevant to the role.

Prefer employer announcements, official filings, original publications, and direct source material. Use credible independent reporting to add or challenge context where available. Verify dates and distinguish current developments from historical background. Company statements are company statements; do not turn promotional language into established scientific efficacy or commercial success.

Label each explanatory finding:

| Label | Required support |
|---|---|
| Employer-stated hiring reason | A source explicitly connects this opening or clearly identified hiring campaign to the event |
| Relevant context | The event is sourced, but its connection to the opening is not established |
| Inference | State the reasoning and supporting facts while clearly marking uncertainty |
| Unavailable | State that no supported explanation was found within the recorded research scope |

Do not present proximity in time as causation. Do not claim a job is backfill, newly funded, urgent, or created by an expansion without support. A full research attempt can end with sparse evidence; disclose the limits rather than fill the brief with speculation.

## Deliver and preserve

Save job records, source evidence, company briefs, and the search-run record. Present a ranked table with employer/title/location, score, unknown share, strongest evidence, material gaps/conflicts, verified posting/deadline information, and the canonical link. Provide the full company brief for every qualifying match as linked or accompanying local research material; do not quietly reduce deep research to a one-line note.

Make the method and limitations accessible without overwhelming the shortlist. Report sources not checked, jobs whose full JD was unavailable, and work remaining beyond the batch. Let the applicant choose openings before tailoring application materials. Recheck availability and JD changes before tailoring and again before portal entry; preserve the relevant JD version for approval.
